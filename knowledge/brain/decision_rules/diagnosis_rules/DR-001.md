# DR-001 -- Lack of spatial anchor

## 1. Rule ID

DR-001

## 2. Rule Name

Lack of spatial anchor

## 3. Applicable Scenario

Space is clean but lacks attraction.

## 4. Observation Conditions

The site "s Cognition Record shows at least two of:

- Scattered facilities, no centre.
- No visual centre.
- No behaviour centre.
- Multiple competing small features at similar weight.

This is the binding observation for the Diagnosis
family verdict `focal = absent` or `focal =
competing` (see `knowledge/brain/diagnosis/README.md`).

## 5. Judgment

The space lacks a core.

## 6. Reasoning

Space is not an equipment collection. Without an
anchor, the eye does not know where to land, the body
does not know where to go, and the story does not know
where to start.

This is the design principle: one core, not many. The
absence of an anchor is the most common reason a
"clean" space feels empty.

## 7. Recommended Action

- Remove low-value elements first (the "remove before
  add" rule).
- Create a single spatial anchor: a landmark, a node,
  a view, a tree, a wall, a roof. The anchor may be
  existing (Anchor strategy) or new (Landmark
  strategy).

## 8. Exceptions

The rule may not fire if the project type requires
"field" character (a meadow, a wetland boardwalk,
landscape-only). In that case, "no anchor" is
intentional, and the diagnosis verdict is `focal =
none -- by design`.

## 9. Example Cases

- A new playground opens with eight small structures
  spread evenly across the lawn; children wander
  between them without committing; DR-001 fires.
- A renovated courtyard has a beautiful existing
  sycamore; the new design adds a small bench under it
  and removes the central fountain that competed for
  attention; DR-001 no longer fires.
