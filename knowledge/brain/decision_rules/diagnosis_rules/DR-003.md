# DR-003 -- Visual attraction does not equal play value

## 1. Rule ID

DR-003

## 2. Rule Name

Visual attraction does not equal play value

## 3. Applicable Scenario

Space looks beautiful but children leave quickly.

## 4. Observation Conditions

The Experience Profile shows:

- Stay desire at first sight is high.
- Repeat visits or dwell time observed as low.
- Single function across the space.
- Weak interaction between user and the objects.

This is the binding observation for the Experience
Profile verdict `vitality = dormant` and for the
Diagnosis verdict `vitality = dormant` together with
`focal = misdirected`.

## 5. Judgment

Experience depth is insufficient.

## 6. Reasoning

A space can be photographed beautifully and still fail
the user. Play value is **measured by what the body
and the mind do**, not by what the eye sees.

This is the binding of Decision Principle 002 (Space
before object). The space, not the object, must
perform.

## 7. Recommended Action

Increase:

- **Challenge** -- physical or cognitive difficulty.
- **Cooperation** -- multi-person use, social play.
- **Exploration** -- partial hiding, partial
  discovery, surprises.

A common intervention: introduce a Journey strategy
even when the surface is clean. A clean but lifeless
space needs *path*, not *objects*.

## 8. Exceptions

The rule may not fire if the project type is explicitly
visual (e.g., a public square whose primary metric is
photo share). In that case vitality is a different KPI
and DR-003 "s challenge/cooperation/exploration
recipe is optional, not mandatory.

## 9. Example Cases

- A polished public sculpture looks striking but does
  not invite touch or sitting; DR-003 fires.
- A clean kindergarten yard has one climbing structure,
  a slide, a sandbox; children wander and leave; DR-
  003 fires. Adding a den, a hidden path, and a
  cooperation game raises play value without changing
  the photo.
