# DR-002 -- Existing anchor but lack of coherence

## 1. Rule ID

DR-002

## 2. Rule Name

Existing anchor but lack of coherence

## 3. Applicable Scenario

Space has facilities but feels ordinary.

## 4. Observation Conditions

The site "s Experience Profile and Cognition Record
show:

- Mixed styles across facilities.
- Inconsistent colours.
- Fragmented themes (multiple themes competing).
- No unifying material or pattern language.

This is the binding observation for the Diagnosis
family verdict `focal = misdirected` or
`focal = competing`.

## 5. Judgment

Theme and visual language are not unified.

The anchor exists; the language around it is broken.

## 6. Reasoning

A space without a language reads as ordinary even when
it has good objects. This is the binding of Expert
Handbook rule "do not pile on equipment; design the
story first" (`02_Expert_Rules.md`).

## 7. Recommended Action

- Keep valuable assets (the anchor + assets that
  extend it).
- Remove disruptive elements (anything that competes
  with the anchor or breaks the unifying language).
- Create coherence by introducing one unifying move
  (one material family, one colour family, one pattern
  motif, one story spine).

## 8. Exceptions

The rule may not fire if the project type is
intentionally polyglot (e.g., a world-culture park
where each zone has a different language). In that
case coherence is per-zone, not site-wide.

## 9. Example Cases

- A school playground has a wooden climbing structure
  (the anchor) plus a plastic rocket climber plus a
  steel pirate ship; DR-002 fires -- three languages
  fight; the rocket and pirate ship are disruptive.
- A kindergarten courtyard has a single tree-house
  motif extended in wood, rope, and earth-tone
  fabrics; DR-002 does not fire -- language is
  coherent.
