# Treehouse

> **Object ID:** `OBJECT.TREEHOUSE` — **Label:** Treehouse（树屋）
> **Category:** STRUCTURE / ELEVATED_PLATFORM
> **Domain Affinity:** PLAYGROUND, NATURE_PLAY, ECO_RESORT, EDUCATIONAL

## 1. Definition

An elevated, cabin-like play and rest structure that gives children (or adults) a sense of being up in the canopy. Often built from wood or wood+steel, it can be a passive lookout, a story venue, or the anchor of a larger playground. The "treehouse" metaphor is a powerful narrative device, not a literal tree requirement.

## 2. Spatial Requirements

- Minimum footprint: 4 m x 4 m ground area, 6 m x 6 m including safety clear zone.
- Recommended footprint: 6 m x 6 m platform with 8 m x 8 m clear zone.
- Vertical clearance: 2.5 m under the lowest soffit for safe use; 3.0 m preferred.
- Height to platform: 1.8 m to 3.5 m. Above 3.5 m requires engineered fall protection and impact-attenuating surfacing.
- Required clear zone: 2 m around the structure, free of hard obstacles and moving equipment.

## 3. Capacity & User Fit

- Typical capacity: 6 to 10 children on the platform at one time, plus 2 adults.
- Recommended age range: 3 to 12 years (low platforms); 5 to 12 years (high platforms).
- Group configuration: small group storytelling, paired climbing, or single-child lookout.
- Adult accompaniment: required below age 6; encouraged up to age 9 for high platforms.

## 4. Materials & Construction

- Primary materials: Robinia (natural wood), Glulam timber, or steel frame with timber cladding.
- Secondary materials: HDPE panels, rope nets, stainless steel fixings, polycarbonate glazing.
- Construction type: post-and-beam with engineered footings; not actually attached to living trees.
- Lifespan estimate: 15 to 20 years for natural wood, 25+ years for steel frame with timber skin.
- Climate suitability: outdoor; natural wood needs UV and moisture protection; steel frame tolerates coastal climates.

## 5. Safety & Standards

- Applicable safety standards: EN 1176 (playground equipment), EN 1177 (surfacing), local building code for elevated structures.
- Critical risks: falls from height, entrapment in gaps, head entrapment in railings.
- Required safety surface: impact-attenuating surface (rubber, engineered wood fibre, sand) within the fall zone.
- Inclusive design considerations: ramp or alternative transfer route to platform; tactile wayfinding; seating inside the treehouse for non-climbers.

## 6. Behavior Affordances

- Primary behaviors enabled: observation, hide-and-seek, socialising, imaginative role play.
- Secondary behaviors enabled: light climbing (stairs/ladder), reading, counting nature.
- Social configuration: small cooperative group; often co-uses with a slide or rope bridge.
- Sensory profile: visual (long sightlines), tactile (wood grain), mild vestibular (height), acoustic (dampened below platform).

## 7. Cost & Lifecycle

- Indicative cost band: medium to high. Region- and material-dependent; treat any single number as a placeholder.
- Installation complexity: high. Engineered foundation, crane lift for assembled modules, site access matters.
- Maintenance cycle: annual timber inspection; bi-annual hardware torque check; re-coat every 2 to 3 years.
- Replacement cycle: panels and ropes earlier than structure; full refurbishment at year 12 to 15.
- Spare part availability: dependent on fabricator; specify stocked spare parts in the original contract.

## 8. Suitable Space Contexts

- Best site types: nature parks, resort grounds, large public parks, school grounds, eco-tourism sites.
- Climate/exposure fit: temperate and subtropical outdoor climates; avoid exposed coastal or extreme alpine without design adaptation.
- Surrounding intensity: best in calm to medium-activity contexts. Competes with loud equipment if placed too close.
- Companion infrastructure: shade trees, soft pathways, signage, accessible approach route, drainage.

## 9. Theme Affinities

- Strong fit themes: Nature (forest, jungle, mountain, garden, river), Castle (tower reading), Fantasy (fairy tale cottage, elf roost).
- Possible fit themes: Animal (den, burrow, nest), Pirate (crow's nest), Space (control tower in some storytelling).
- Themes to avoid: deep-ocean, modern industrial sci-fi, minimalist contemporary contexts where the structure reads as a theme prop instead of architecture.
- Cultural/contextual notes: treehouse imagery resonates strongly in cultures with forest heritage (Northern Europe, Northeast Asia, parts of North America). Less familiar where the built environment is purely urban.

## 10. Adjacent Object Relationships

- Pairs well with: Slide (OBJECT.SLIDE) as a return-to-ground element, Rope bridge for connection between treehouses, Reading_Corner at the base for cross-generational use.
- Conflicts with: very loud adjacent equipment; bare modern minimalism that reads as visual mismatch.
- Replaces: standalone lookout deck; freestanding bench-shelter.
- Replaced by: amphitheatre (for larger audiences), platform tower with net (for higher density use).

## 11. Decision Notes

- When to recommend: site has footprint >= 6 m x 6 m, the user wants a strong narrative anchor, and the surrounding landscape can carry a wooden/earthy visual language.
- When NOT to recommend: site is small (< 4 m x 4 m), budget cannot support engineered foundations, or the brief demands a pure minimalist look.
- Open questions for the Decision Engine: how is "footprint" measured (ground area, total envelope, or platform area)? How is "inclusivity" scored when the user does not specify?
