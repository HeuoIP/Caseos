# Slide

> **Object ID:** `OBJECT.SLIDE` — **Label:** Slide（滑梯）
> **Category:** MOVEMENT / DESCENT
> **Domain Affinity:** PLAYGROUND, WATER_PLAY, ADVENTURE, COMMERCIAL_FAMILY_VENUE

## 1. Definition

A descent element that returns the user to the ground from a higher level. Slides are universal across cultures and age groups and come in many forms: straight, spiral, tube, embankment, roller, double, and sculptural. A slide is often the primary "destination" object in a play sequence and a major visual landmark.

## 2. Spatial Requirements

- Minimum footprint: 2 m wide runout + 2 m wide start zone, length driven by height.
- Recommended footprint: 4 m wide x 8 m long for a 3 m high slide; longer for embankment variants.
- Vertical clearance: 2.0 m above slide centerline; more for spiral and tube slides.
- Required clear zone: 2 m at the exit, 1.5 m on the sides, no obstacles in the runout.

## 3. Capacity & User Fit

- Typical capacity: one child at a time per lane; queues form at the top.
- Recommended age range: 2 to 12 years for most forms; embankment slides can serve all ages.
- Group configuration: solo descent; social configuration is the queue, not the ride.
- Adult accompaniment: required below age 4; encouraged up to age 6.

## 4. Materials & Construction

- Primary materials: stainless steel (most common), HDPE (rotational moulded), glass-fibre reinforced plastic (FRP) for sculptural, wood for embankment.
- Secondary materials: rubber surfacing at runout, steel or timber stairs, handrails.
- Construction type: factory-fabricated chute + site-installed stairs and supports; or fully site-built for embankment.
- Lifespan estimate: 20+ years for stainless; 10 to 15 for HDPE; 15+ for FRP.
- Climate suitability: stainless handles freeze-thaw; HDPE may warp under sustained heat; FRP needs UV protection.

## 5. Safety & Standards

- Applicable safety standards: EN 1176 (slides specifically addressed in part 3), EN 1177 surfacing, ASTM F1487.
- Critical risks: falls from the top, slide-out collisions, hand/arm entrapment on side rails, overheating on metal slides in full sun.
- Required safety surface: impact-attenuating material at runout for the height of the slide.
- Inclusive design considerations: alternative transfer platform at the top, wide chute for assisted use, tactile entry cues, shaded slide surface where climate requires.

## 6. Behavior Affordances

- Primary behaviors enabled: sliding, descent thrill, queuing, climbing back up.
- Secondary behaviors enabled: social waiting, observation of others, mild risk calibration.
- Social configuration: sequential rather than parallel; group behaviour lives in the queue and the climb back up.
- Sensory profile: vestibular (descent), tactile (surface temperature), acoustic (whoosh), visual (height).

## 7. Cost & Lifecycle

- Indicative cost band: low to medium for straight slides; medium to high for spiral and sculptural; site-built embankment is medium.
- Installation complexity: medium. Prefabricated slides need a crane; site-built slides need formwork and finishing.
- Maintenance cycle: monthly inspection of side rails, daily removal of debris, quarterly hardware check.
- Replacement cycle: 10 to 15 years for HDPE before colour fade and warping; 20+ for stainless before repolishing.
- Spare part availability: standard for stainless and HDPE; specialised for sculptural FRP.

## 8. Suitable Space Contexts

- Best site types: public parks, schoolyards, family entertainment venues, resort pools, large residential developments.
- Climate/exposure fit: stainless handles most climates; HDPE needs shade in hot regions; FRP needs UV-stable paint in sunny climates.
- Surrounding intensity: tolerates high activity; benefits from a clear approach and runout.
- Companion infrastructure: shade structure over the runout in hot climates, accessible transfer path, clear sightlines from seating for caregivers.

## 9. Theme Affinities

- Strong fit themes: Pirate (rope rigging), Space (rocket slide), Castle (turret slide), Animal (creature slide), Transportation (carriage slide), Dragon (spine slide).
- Possible fit themes: Nature (embankment slide on a slope), Deep_Sea (submarine tube), Industrial sci-fi (sleek metal).
- Themes to avoid: calm library or reading-only contexts; minimal contemporary sites where the slide reads as out of place.
- Cultural/contextual notes: slide acceptance is near-universal. Length and height tolerances vary by jurisdiction and cultural risk appetite.

## 10. Adjacent Object Relationships

- Pairs well with: Treehouse (top of slide as exit from treehouse), Climbing structure (the way back up), Interactive_Wall (queue-time engagement).
- Conflicts with: very narrow approaches; no-runout designs.
- Replaces: the descent function of generic elevated decks.
- Replaced by: spiral staircases, ramps, or zip-lines in contexts where sliding is not appropriate.

## 11. Decision Notes

- When to recommend: the user has age 2 to 12 users, there is a defined elevated structure or slope, and there is space for a safe runout.
- When NOT to recommend: site cannot absorb the runout, surface material is incompatible (e.g. concrete), or the brief is fully inclusive wheelchair-only and no alternative descent exists.
- Open questions for the Decision Engine: how are jurisdictional height limits encoded? How is "inclusivity" weighted against the desire for thrill?
