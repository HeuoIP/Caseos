# Reading Corner

> **Object ID:** `OBJECT.READING_CORNER` — **Label:** Reading Corner（阅读角）
> **Category:** REST / STORYTELLING / INCLUSION
> **Domain Affinity:** PLAYGROUND, EDUCATION, COMMUNITY, TRADITIONAL_CULTURE

## 1. Definition

A sheltered, low-arousal seating area designed for reading, storytelling, conversation, or quiet observation. It can be a small pavilion, a built-in bench nook, a hedge-enclosed clearing, or a softscape amphitheatre. It is the most cost-effective inclusion object in the entire library and a strong cross-generational anchor.

## 2. Spatial Requirements

- Minimum footprint: 3 m x 3 m seating area + 1.5 m circulation around.
- Recommended footprint: 5 m x 5 m with integrated planting, signage, and a small rug or mat.
- Vertical clearance: 2.1 m under any roof; no roof is acceptable if shade and weather protection come from trees.
- Height: ground level, optionally on a low platform (under 0.4 m) for definition.
- Required clear zone: 1 m around the seating for access and strollers.

## 3. Capacity & User Fit

- Typical capacity: 4 to 8 children plus 2 to 4 adults.
- Recommended age range: all ages. Works especially well for 0 to 6 plus caregivers, and for 6 to 12 quiet activity.
- Group configuration: small intimate group, story circle, parent-child reading.
- Adult accompaniment: encouraged for all ages; central to the use case.

## 4. Materials & Construction

- Primary materials: timber bench seating, stone or concrete pad, shade structure (timber, steel, or natural canopy).
- Secondary materials: bookshelves or weatherproof book-box, soft flooring (rubber mat, decking, or sand), signage.
- Construction type: simple carpentry and paving; very low technical risk.
- Lifespan estimate: 15+ years for timber and stone; 8 to 12 for soft flooring.
- Climate suitability: outdoor; needs rain protection for books and shade for users.

## 5. Safety & Standards

- Applicable safety standards: general public space safety; EN 1176 only if it contains fixed play equipment.
- Critical risks: trip hazards on uneven paving, very low risk otherwise.
- Required safety surface: not required; accessible, stable, slip-resistant paving is sufficient.
- Inclusive design considerations: benches with backrests and armrests at varying heights; wheelchair turning circle; tactile and high-contrast signage; braille labels on book boxes.

## 6. Behavior Affordances

- Primary behaviors enabled: reading, listening, storytelling, social conversation, rest.
- Secondary behaviors enabled: drawing, board games, observation of nearby activity.
- Social configuration: small group circle, paired parent-child, solo reader.
- Sensory profile: low-arousal, quiet acoustic environment, often close to planting for scent and shade.

## 7. Cost & Lifecycle

- Indicative cost band: low. The least expensive object in the library.
- Installation complexity: low. Standard carpentry and paving.
- Maintenance cycle: monthly clean; quarterly check of timber; re-stain every 2 to 3 years.
- Replacement cycle: paving 20+ years; timber 15; soft flooring 8 to 12; book rotation annual.
- Spare part availability: standard timber and paving; book supply is ongoing community work.

## 8. Suitable Space Contexts

- Best site types: public parks, schoolyards, library gardens, hospital healing gardens, community centres, plazas.
- Climate/exposure fit: all climates if sheltered; semi-outdoor in temperate regions; indoor-outdoor in extreme climates.
- Surrounding intensity: best in low to medium intensity contexts. Can act as a "calm island" inside a busy site.
- Companion infrastructure: shade, accessible path, lighting for evening use, book-exchange program or community library partnership.

## 9. Theme Affinities

- Strong fit themes: Nature (garden, forest, river, mountain), Traditional Culture (pavilion, festival, folk story), Library, Education.
- Possible fit themes: Animal (when the story setting is calm), Castle (reading tower), Fairy_Tale (storytelling cottage).
- Themes to avoid: high-action, loud, or fast-paced themes where the reading corner would be underused.
- Cultural/contextual notes: reading-corner concepts adapt to local reading culture: a Chinese 凉亭 (pavilion), a Japanese 縁側 (engawa), a Nordic biblioteks-trädgård (library garden).

## 10. Adjacent Object Relationships

- Pairs well with: Treehouse (story exit point), Planting (sensory and shade), Sand pit (quiet alternative for very young children), Lighting (evening reading).
- Conflicts with: very loud equipment within 5 m; busy circulation paths crossing through.
- Replaces: the social-rest function of a generic bench.
- Replaced by: amphitheatre (for larger audiences), library building (for indoor depth).

## 11. Decision Notes

- When to recommend: nearly always. It is the lowest-cost, highest-inclusion, most-cross-generational object in the library.
- When NOT to recommend: site is purely a thrill or sports venue with no rest need; site is so small that one seating area consumes too much footprint.
