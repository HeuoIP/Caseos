# Interactive Wall

> **Object ID:** `OBJECT.INTERACTIVE_WALL` — **Label:** Interactive Wall（互动墙）
> **Category:** ENGAGEMENT / MULTI_SENSORY / INCLUSION
> **Domain Affinity:** EDUCATION, MUSEUM, SCIENCE, PLAYGROUND, INDOOR_FAMILY_VENUE

## 1. Definition

A vertical or near-vertical panel with tactile, mechanical, or digital elements that respond to user input. Forms include mechanical marble runs, musical chimes, gear walls, light-and-sound walls, projection walls, and sensor-driven games. The interactive wall is the strongest "all ages, all abilities" engagement object in the library.

## 2. Spatial Requirements

- Minimum footprint: 2 m wide x 0.6 m deep, with 1.5 m circulation in front.
- Recommended footprint: 4 m wide x 1.2 m deep, with 2 m circulation in front, multi-sided for higher throughput.
- Vertical clearance: 2.0 m minimum wall height; 2.4 m preferred.
- Wall height: 0.6 m to 2.0 m; lower elements for seated or wheelchair users, upper for standing adults.
- Required clear zone: 1.5 m in front for circulation, queue, and wheelchair turning.

## 3. Capacity & User Fit

- Typical capacity: 2 to 4 simultaneous users (depending on number of stations); 6 to 10 users cycling in a 5-minute window.
- Recommended age range: 3 to 99. With seat-height access, usable from 18 months with caregiver.
- Group configuration: parallel; multiple users at once with little queuing.
- Adult accompaniment: encouraged below age 5; co-use is part of the value proposition.

## 4. Materials & Construction

- Primary materials: HDPE panels, stainless steel frames, marine plywood, acrylic screens for digital variants.
- Secondary materials: mechanical components (gears, levers, marbles, bellows), electronic components (sensors, speakers, microcontrollers), protective coatings.
- Construction type: factory-fabricated modules assembled on site. Electronics must be IP-rated for outdoor use.
- Lifespan estimate: 10 to 15 years for mechanical; 8 to 12 for electronic due to component wear.
- Climate suitability: outdoor variants need weatherproofing; indoor or covered placement strongly preferred for electronics.

## 5. Safety & Standards

- Applicable safety standards: EN 1176 (playground), EN 62115 (electric toy safety) for powered variants, local low-voltage electrical code.
- Critical risks: pinch points on mechanical parts, electrical safety on powered variants, glass/acrylic breakage, sharp edges on damaged panels.
- Required safety surface: not required (the wall is the engagement surface).
- Inclusive design considerations: seated-height stations, high-contrast colour, tactile cues, audio descriptions, audio induction loop, multilingual text, and visible icons.

## 6. Behavior Affordances

- Primary behaviors enabled: cause-and-effect exploration, collaborative play, problem solving, sensory play.
- Secondary behaviors enabled: language development, scientific observation, music making, drawing and tracing.
- Social configuration: parallel (multiple users) and cooperative (passing objects, taking turns).
- Sensory profile: tactile, acoustic, visual, sometimes proprioceptive. Calmer than thrill equipment.

## 7. Cost & Lifecycle

- Indicative cost band: medium. Mechanical variants cheaper; digital and projection variants significantly more expensive.
- Installation complexity: medium. Requires electrical supply for powered variants, data connection for some digital variants, foundation for free-standing walls.
- Maintenance cycle: weekly clean, monthly mechanical check, quarterly electronic diagnostics, annual firmware update.
- Replacement cycle: 5 to 8 years for electronic components; 10 to 15 for mechanical; panels and structures longer.
- Spare part availability: depends on fabricator. Lock in spares contractually.

## 8. Suitable Space Contexts

- Best site types: science centres, museums, family entertainment venues, schoolyards, public parks in dense urban contexts, hospital and therapy gardens.
- Climate/exposure fit: indoor or covered outdoor. Full outdoor exposure is possible with marine-grade components but raises maintenance.
- Surrounding intensity: medium to high. Provides structured engagement in busy contexts.
- Companion infrastructure: power supply, drainage, network (for some variants), accessible path, lighting for evening use, shade for outdoor placement.

## 9. Theme Affinities

- Strong fit themes: Science (laboratory, robot, AI), Space (control panel, mission console), Education, Museum.
- Possible fit themes: Transportation (vehicle dashboard), Festival (festive panels), Deep_Sea (bioluminescent wall).
- Themes to avoid: nature, traditional culture, fairy tale, and castle themes where the wall's modern mechanical language breaks the immersive atmosphere.
- Cultural/contextual notes: language and content of the wall must be localised. Symbols and audio are more universally accessible than text.

## 10. Adjacent Object Relationships

- Pairs well with: Reading_Corner (deeper dive after exploration), Slide (queue engagement), Treehouse (control-room narrative).
- Conflicts with: open natural landscapes where the modern language dominates; quiet zones.
- Replaces: the educational function of generic signage.
- Replaced by: instructor-led workshops, downloadable digital content, full digital exhibits.

## 11. Decision Notes

- When to recommend: the user prioritises inclusion, multi-age engagement, educational value, and dwell time over physical activity.
- When NOT to recommend: site has no power, no maintenance budget for electronics, or the brief demands a fully natural or historic atmosphere.
- Open questions for the Decision Engine: how is "inclusivity" weighted against theme fit? How is a powered object scored against maintenance cost?
