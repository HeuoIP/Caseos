# IP Sculpture

> **Object ID:** `OBJECT.IP_SCULPTURE` — **Label:** IP Sculpture（IP 雕塑）
> **Category:** LANDMARK / BRANDING / THEME_ANCHOR
> **Domain Affinity:** PLAYGROUND, COMMERCIAL, THEME_PARK, PUBLIC_ART, REAL_ESTATE_MARKETING

## 1. Definition

A large, themed sculptural object that anchors a space visually and narratively. It can be a licensed character, an original themed figure, an abstract landmark, or a scaled environmental feature (e.g. a giant frog, a rocket, a coral reef). The IP sculpture is the strongest "I am here, this is what this place is" object in the library.

## 2. Spatial Requirements

- Minimum footprint: 3 m x 3 m base, with 3 m circulation around.
- Recommended footprint: 6 m x 6 m base, with 5 m clear zone, sightline management to other objects.
- Vertical clearance: depends on the sculpture. Typically 3 m to 8 m tall; very tall sculptures need aviation and structural sign-off.
- Height: any. 4 m to 6 m is the most common for playground contexts.
- Required clear zone: 3 m around the sculpture for viewing, photography, and safe circulation. More if the sculpture is climbable.

## 3. Capacity & User Fit

- Typical capacity: passive viewing unlimited. If the sculpture is climbable or interactive, capacity follows its design.
- Recommended age range: all ages for viewing. Climbable sculptures typically 5 to 12.
- Group configuration: photo stops, group gathering, family selfies.
- Adult accompaniment: required only for very young children near large landmark forms.

## 4. Materials & Construction

- Primary materials: FRP (fibreglass reinforced plastic), stainless steel, glass-fibre concrete, carved foam over steel armature.
- Secondary materials: steel internal frame, paint or gelcoat finish, lighting, integrated seating or play features.
- Construction type: artist-designed, engineered, factory-fabricated, crane-installed on site-built foundation.
- Lifespan estimate: 15 to 25 years; gelcoat and paint refresh every 5 to 8 years.
- Climate suitability: outdoor. Coastal climates need marine-grade finishes; alpine climates need freeze-thaw-rated concrete.

## 5. Safety & Standards

- Applicable safety standards: local structural code, EN 1176 only if the sculpture is a play structure, public art safety standards in some jurisdictions.
- Critical risks: fall from climbable sculpture, structural failure (rare but high-consequence), entrapment in openings, sharp edges.
- Required safety surface: depends on accessibility. If the sculpture is climbable, full EN 1177 impact-attenuating surfacing within the fall zone.
- Inclusive design considerations: tactile replicas or scale models for visually impaired users, seated viewing positions, clear ground-level interpretation, multilingual signage.

## 6. Behavior Affordances

- Primary behaviors enabled: viewing, photography, social meeting at a landmark, wayfinding.
- Secondary behaviors enabled: climbing (if designed for it), role play, storytelling, brand association.
- Social configuration: focal point of larger social configurations; people gather around it, not within it.
- Sensory profile: visual dominant, tactile if climbable, acoustic (sculpture can host speakers or chimes).

## 7. Cost & Lifecycle

- Indicative cost band: medium to high. Wide range driven by size, artist fees, and finish quality. Treat any single number as a placeholder.
- Installation complexity: high. Civil foundation, crane lift, artist supervision on site, structural sign-off.
- Maintenance cycle: quarterly visual inspection, annual structural check, repaint every 5 to 8 years, lighting maintenance as needed.
- Replacement cycle: 15 to 25 years before major refurbishment; full replacement is rare.
- Spare part availability: artwork-specific. Document the design, moulds, and finishes contractually.

## 8. Suitable Space Contexts

- Best site types: theme parks, branded family venues, real-estate marketing plazas, large public parks, commercial family destinations, urban landmarks.
- Climate/exposure fit: outdoor; tolerates most climates with appropriate finish specification.
- Surrounding intensity: works in calm and busy sites, but the clear zone and sightlines must be protected.
- Companion infrastructure: lighting (uplighting for night viewing), photo spots, signage, accessible approach, drainage, anti-graffiti coating.

## 9. Theme Affinities

- Strong fit themes: any themed context where the sculpture IS the character or landmark (Animal, Space, Castle, Pirate, Transportation, Dragon, Robot).
- Possible fit themes: Nature (naturalistic sculptures of large animals or plants), Traditional Culture (cultural figure), Festival (festive installation).
- Themes to avoid: pure nature conservation contexts, minimalist contemporary contexts, sacred or contemplative spaces.
- Cultural/contextual notes: licensed IP requires rights clearance, royalty agreements, and brand-guideline compliance. Culturally sensitive subjects need community review.

## 10. Adjacent Object Relationships

- Pairs well with: Slide (the sculpture as the top of the slide), Treehouse (sculpture as a feature at the platform), Interactive_Wall (sculpture as the visual anchor, wall as the activity).
- Conflicts with: other large visual objects competing for the same sightline.
- Replaces: the visual landmark function of generic signage or pylons.
- Replaced by: architectural landmark, large natural feature (mountain, tree), branded gatehouse.

## 11. Decision Notes

- When to recommend: the brief explicitly requires a landmark, a brand anchor, a photo destination, or a strong visual identity. Budget supports artist design and engineered installation.
- When NOT to recommend: the site is brand-sensitive, the budget is constrained, the context demands pure nature, or the user prefers minimalist contemporary design.
- Open questions for the Decision Engine: how is licensed IP scored against original art? How is a sculpture's "iconic value" quantified?
